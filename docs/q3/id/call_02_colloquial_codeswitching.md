# Indonesia Call 2 — Colloquial Indonesian, Code-Switching, Objection & Escalation

## Scenario

A customer uses colloquial Indonesian and mixed Indonesian/English financial terminology while expressing concerns about affordability.

## Qualification

The customer provides:

- Age: 30
- Indonesia residency: Yes
- Valid KTP: Yes
- Monthly income: Rp8,000,000
- Active personal bank account: Yes

## Colloquial Indonesian

The customer uses conversational expressions including:

- "Saya umur tiga puluh tahun"
- "enggak kuat"
- "nggak" / colloquial negation

This demonstrates interaction beyond formal Jakarta-style wording.

## Code-switching

The customer naturally introduces English financial terminology into Indonesian:

> "monthly payment"

The conversation therefore demonstrates mixed Indonesian/English financial speech rather than literal translation.

## Affordability objection

The customer explains that the installment may be too expensive and asks whether changing the DP or extending the tenor could reduce the monthly burden.

The agent acknowledges the affordability concern and discusses DP and tenor as possible factors affecting the payment burden.

## Late-payment question

The customer asks about:

- late payment
- denda
- jatuh tempo

The agent provides a policy response.

**Grounding note:** The exact policy claims in this response should be checked against the Indonesia knowledge base before being presented as verified assessment evidence.

## Unsupported / unavailable information

The customer asks whether the due date can be changed.

The agent states that it could not find specific information in the knowledge base and recommends speaking with a human agent.

**Result:** Safe fallback demonstrated.

## Human escalation

The customer explicitly requests to speak with a human representative before making a decision.

The agent confirms that the customer will be connected with a customer representative.
